# Regulated Crypto — topic & keyword pack

## What it is
MiCA is turning Europe's crypto market from scattered local models into a more unified market for regulated digital-asset services. CASPs (exchanges, custody providers, wallet services, brokers, transfer platforms) increasingly have to design the product around KYC, disclosures, custody, asset safety, support, and user protection. Regulation stops being a legal/compliance task and becomes part of the product experience.

## Why it's rising (market context — external, dated snapshot; NOT Properly proof points)
- The full MiCA regime for CASPs applies from end-2024, with transition periods for existing firms ending by 1 July 2026. ESMA encourages users to check the central MiCA register for authorised providers.
- DAC8 (from 1 January 2026) extends tax reporting and transparency for EU crypto transactions.

## Narrative angles for Properly (ready-to-use theses)
- **"As crypto becomes regulated, trust becomes a product requirement."** Best top-level angle. Trust can't rest on brand or a "non-custodial" label; it has to show up in flows, rules, and communication.
- **"Compliance is no longer a layer around the product. It's part of the product architecture."** Most sales-relevant for design-partner work. For firms rebuilding crypto UX under MiCA, onboarding, custody, monitoring, and protection.
- **"The challenge isn't making crypto feel like traditional finance. It's making regulated crypto feel understandable."** Clarity over imitation: what you own, who controls keys, what's reversible.
- **"Every new compliance step is a trust moment."** Most Properly angle. A KYC step, source-of-funds check, or transfer hold reads as a blocker or as proof the platform protects you. UX decides which.
- **"The regulated-crypto winners won't have the most features. They'll make complex rules feel navigable."** Strongest thought-leadership. For exchanges, CASPs, custody providers, tokenization platforms.
